My grandmother kept a garden behind her house that was far too large for one person to manage, and yet she managed it every year without complaint. She planted tomatoes along the southern fence, where they caught the afternoon sun, and beans that climbed up strings she tied to the gutter. In the middle there was a patch of herbs: basil, thyme, rosemary, and a stubborn clump of mint that spread a little further every spring no matter how often she cut it back.

When I visited in the summer, she would hand me a basket and send me out before breakfast to pick whatever was ripe. I was not very good at it. I pulled up carrots that were still too small and left zucchini on the vine until they were the size of baseball bats. She never scolded me. She would just laugh, hold up the enormous vegetable, and say that we would have to make bread with it again.

Years later, after she had moved into a smaller apartment in town, I asked her whether she missed the garden. She thought about it for a long time before answering. She said she missed the smell of the soil after rain, and the quiet of the early morning, but that what she really missed was having a reason to go outside every day and see what had changed. I think about that often now, whenever I find myself staying indoors for too long.
